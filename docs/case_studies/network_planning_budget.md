# Case Study — From a Planning Spreadsheet to an Auditable Network Planning & Budget Engine

> **Release line:** v2.1 → v2.5 · **Data:** 100% synthetic (`network_v1`, seed 42)
> **Numbers:** generated by the engine — see [`reports/network/network_analytics_summary.md`](../../reports/network/network_analytics_summary.md)

---

## 1. The problem class

Every year, transportation planning teams turn a demand forecast into a fleet plan and a freight budget. The classic tool is a large spreadsheet: weekly forecast by store → volume by route → trips → cost → monthly budget, plus seasonal factors, rate adjustments, extras and taxes.

Spreadsheets solve the problem but carry structural risks:

- assumptions written as literals inside thousands of formulas;
- top-down management adjustments mixed into the calculation, invisible later;
- no plan-vs-actual decomposition, so variances are explained by opinion;
- peaks that exceed the planned frequency, with no cost attached;
- no way to compare scenarios without duplicating files.

This project rebuilds that **problem class** — not any real spreadsheet — as a tested analytics engine inside the AI Supply Chain Copilot.

## 2. What was built

| Layer | Capability |
|---|---|
| Data (v2.1) | Seeded generator: 3 DCs, 120 stores, 29 routes (standard, two-driver, alternating-week, air), 148 weeks of store-level shipments, planner-style budget forecasts, 5.7k executed trips, scenarios, parameters, management adjustments |
| Forecast (v2.2) | Seasonal naïve, moving average, Holt-Winters; rolling backtest; WAPE / bias / FVA by store, route, DC |
| Planning (v2.3) | Monthly fixed frequency (MEAN / P75 / MAX) + weekly overflow priced at spot; what-if without touching the baseline; consolidation candidates |
| Budget (v2.4) | Civil-month budget lines by DC × route × component; **approved management adjustments as separate, traceable lines**; exact volume / efficiency / rate bridge; plan vs actual |
| Sustainability (v2.5) | GHG estimate per trip / route / piece (illustrative factors) |
| AI layer | 16 allow-listed capabilities routed by a Pydantic contract; the LLM never computes numbers |

## 3. Findings the engine surfaces (synthetic data)

1. **Optimistic forecasts cost money through fixed capacity.** The 2026 budget forecast carried a +3.1% bias. Actual 2026 YTD volume came in below plan, and the variance decomposition shows the *volume* saving was largely eaten by a worse *efficiency* effect: trips sized for the forecast ran emptier.
2. **Sizing for the peak is more expensive than paying spot.** Moving from a MEAN-based monthly frequency to MAX removes every spot trip but adds ~R$ 0.56M and ~111 tCO₂e to the 2027 budget. P75 is a middle ground.
3. **A route can stay in the budget after its only store closes.** One alternating-week route has zero 2027 demand; the planner flags it instead of budgeting empty trucks.
4. **Low-utilization routes have a cost-vs-service trade-off.** Lowering the minimum frequency on one medium route saves ~R$ 88k per year, but the engine labels it `LOWER_MIN_FREQUENCY`: a service-level decision that stays human.
5. **Management adjustments are visible.** The 2027 budget shows the model baseline, a −3% top-down efficiency target and a DC contingency as separate lines with reason, requester and approver. A pending adjustment is listed but never applied.
6. **Extras were over-budgeted.** The plan assumed 4% of freight; actual extras ran at roughly 2.5%.
7. **Air is ~30% of emissions** for a small share of volume (illustrative factors).

## 4. Design decisions

- **Rebuild the problem, not the spreadsheet** (clean-room rules in `06_transportation_architecture.md`).
- **Parameters are data.** Frequencies, extras, tax credits, thresholds and rate adjustments live in `tn_parameters` with scope and validity.
- **Adjustments are records, not formulas.** Removing them returns exactly the model budget (tested).
- **Exact decomposition.** `C = V × (T/V) × (C/T)`; steps sum to the variance with zero residual (tested). Air routes are priced per piece, so their efficiency effect is zero by construction.
- **LLM ≠ calculator.** The router can only pick allow-listed capabilities and typed fields; what-if overrides are built deterministically by the dispatcher.

## 5. Limits

- Synthetic data: findings illustrate the method, not any real operation.
- No optimization solver (by design, ADR-022).
- Emission factors are illustrative.
- The engine models a plan year by ISO weeks; boundary weeks fall into adjacent civil months.
- The Power BI report is not yet built; `output/network/*.csv` is the ready data model.
